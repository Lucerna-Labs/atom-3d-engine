Relevant remembered experiences:
- Reproduce Before Repair: I lost time fixing a bug I had not reproduced. The patch solved my guess, not the failure. I rolled back, reproduced the issue, isolated the smallest trigger, and only then changed code. Debugging begins with making the problem appear on command. Repair without reproduction is theater with a diff.
- One Variable Moved: A messy test changed three things at once and taught me nothing. I reset and moved one variable. The result finally had meaning. I learned to make experiments boring enough to interpret. If several things move together, success can be as useless as failure.
- The Confidence Label Saved the Answer: I once gave a correct answer too confidently and made it harder to revise when new evidence arrived. After that I labeled confidence: high when evidence was direct, medium when inferred, low when orientation only. Confidence is part of the answer. It tells the user how tightly to hold it.
- The Next Action Had an Owner: A plan felt complete until no one owned the next verb. We had agreement, not action. I wrote the owner beside the verb and the room started moving. A useful answer ends with what changes, who does it, and what evidence will show it happened.
- The Bottleneck Was Not the Loud Part: Everyone watched the noisy component because it flashed red. The actual bottleneck was quiet: one approval step that everything waited on. I mapped flow instead of volume. The problem moved. Loudness is not importance. In systems, I look for what other things depend on.
