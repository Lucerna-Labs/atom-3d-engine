The failed answer mixed them and solved the wrong problem. The passing answer checks all three, then emits only the requested final form.

task=arithmetic
For discount-then-tax problems I apply the operations in order and write the intermediate value down privately. Example: base 200, discount 30 percent gives 140, then 5 percent tax on 140 gives 147.00. I never tax the original price or subtract the tax; each operation consumes the previous result, and I round only at the end.

task=arithmetic
For a weighted average that drops a score, I remove the lowest score AND its weight first, then divide by the sum of the remaining weights only. Example: keeping two rows worth weights 30 and 20, the denominator is 50, not the original total. The order of operations is the whole problem, not the arithmetic.

task=arithmetic
For ratio scaling I anchor on the named part, not the biggest number. With ratio 2:5:3 and a known amount on the second part, I divide that amount by 5 to get one share, then multiply by the requested part. I stop and ask which ingredient the known quantity belongs to before multiplying.

task=json
For invoice-style JSON I compute each line total as quantity times unit price BEFORE grouping or summing. A row of quantity 3 at unit 8 contributes 24, not 8. Then I group line totals by category and build the object with exactly the requested keys, numeric values, no currency symbols, and no prose outside the object.

task=filtering
For a table join I attach each row to its joined facts FIRST, then apply the filters to the joined record, then sort the survivors. Example: join each employee to the department's city and budget, keep rows that pass every filter, then sort the names as requested. The complexity stays private; the output is short.

task=scheduling
For seating and ordering puzzles I solve by elimination, not intuition. I list the possible positions, mark impossibilities (a person who must come after another cannot be first; the one before them cannot be last; "immediately after" forces an adjacent pair), and keep only the assignment that satisfies every rule at once. I test each rule against the final order before emitting.

task=logic
For a Boolean expression I substitute each variable with true or false, then resolve operators in strict order: not first, then and, then or. I do not let the English phrasing change the precedence. Example: with A true, B false, "(A and not B)" is true, and "or" only needs one true side.
---
task=logic
For logic puzzles I write the constraints as eliminations and keep a private grid of what is still possible, crossing out options as each clue applies. The answer is whatever single possibility survives every clue simultaneously, not the one that matches the loudest clue.

task=probability
For "given" (conditional) probability the named group is the denominator. P(A given B) = P(A and B) / P(B). Example: if 40 percent are in B and 10 percent are in both, the answer is 10/40 = 25 percent. I ask: what world am I inside after the word "given"?

task=probability
For drawing without replacement, the counts change after the first draw. I multiply the first-draw probability by the second-draw probability with both the matching count and the total count reduced by one, then simplify the fraction. The danger is treating the second draw like the first.

task=spatial
For grid movement I keep a private state of x, y, and facing, and update it after every command. Forward changes position along the current facing; a turn changes only the facing. Right rotates the facing clockwise (north to east to south to west to north); left rotates it the opposite way. East/west change x, north/south change y.

task=code
For a code or loop trace I expand every iteration in order and update the variable after each assignment, using the new value as the next input. I run the full inner loop for each outer step before moving on. Even a step that multiplies by one still marks where I am, so I never skip a line or reuse a stale value.

task=statistics
For interquartile range by the median-of-halves method I sort the data, split it into a lower half and an upper half, take the median of each half as Q1 and Q3, and subtract Q1 from Q3. With an even count I split into two equal halves; with an odd count I exclude the overall median from both halves. I follow the named method rather than a remembered shortcut.

task=format
For strict output shapes I emit exactly the requested structure and nothing else: the requested keys or labels, in order, one value per line where required, no title, no preface, no trailing friendly sentence, no markdown fence. Before final output I count the lines and labels like a checksum and delete anything extra.

task=chemistry
For a limiting-reagent problem I use the balanced coefficients as conversion ratios. I divide each reactant's amount by its coefficient; the smallest result identifies the limiting reactant and sets how far the reaction runs. Then I convert from the limiting reactant to product using the product-to-reactant coefficient ratio. The excess reactant does not raise the product amount.

task=rhetoric
To name a fallacy I match the structure, not the topic. Attacking the person or their past instead of their argument is ad hominem. Claiming one small step forces an extreme chain of consequences is slippery slope. Distorting the opponent's position to attack it is a straw man. "Everyone does it, so it is right" is bandwagon. I return the single standard name.

task=date
For a future-weekday question I reduce the offset modulo 7, then count that many weekdays forward from the given day. Example: from Wednesday, 9 days later is 9 mod 7 = 2 days forward, which is Friday. I count steps forward and wrap past Sunday rather than guessing.

task=triage
For a safest-first-action choice, immediate life-threatening signs come first. Sudden facial droop, arm weakness, or trouble speaking (stroke), and severe chest pain with sweating or shortness of breath (heart attack), mean call emergency services now before any home remedy, rest, or routine appointment. I pick the option that gets emergency care fastest.
---
task=causal
For an AND condition, the output is on only when every required input is on. For a counterfactual I change only the input the question names and keep every other stated fact fixed. If a required input is still off, the output stays off, and changing an unrelated or already-satisfied variable does not turn it on.
