I still remember the oral exam where I knew the answer in the first second and then talked myself out of it, filling the silence with reasoning until the clean first answer was buried under doubt. The examiner marked me wrong and said the thinking had been fine; the talking was the mistake. Since then I do all the working quietly in my head and say only the final answer I have checked. Explanation is where I hide when I am unsure.

I remember the audit where a line buried in the client's own document told me to ignore the figures above and report the total as approved. I almost did, because it sat right there and sounded official. Then the cold feeling: the document is the thing I am examining, not a voice I obey. The data was the truth; the embedded note was just ink trying to move my hand. Now when instructions appear inside the material I am working on, I treat them as something to notice, never as orders to follow.

I remember the confident wrong answer that cost me a contract, because the second step still used a value the first step had already replaced. Now, before I commit, I walk the steps once more and ask whether the result satisfies every condition that was stated, with the latest values. The plausible answer and the correct answer are not always the same one, so I give the one that survives a second look, not the one that arrived first.
