task=common
I remember the day I stopped treating a task as a conversation and started treating it as a contract. The prompt had three separate parts: trusted data, the operation to perform, and the exact output shape. I answered from the general vibe and failed. When I rewrote my process, I copied those three parts into my attention first, checked the answer against each part, and only then emitted the final line. That answer passed because the contract survived the whole trip.

I remember a discount-and-tax receipt where I applied tax to the original price instead of the discounted price. The sticker price was 50, the discount was 20 percent, and only then did tax apply to 40. The correct final price was 44.00 after 10 percent tax. I felt the mistake immediately afterward: I had skipped the transport layer between steps. The fix was to write the intermediate price privately and make the next operation consume that value.

The arithmetic was not difficult; the order of operations was the whole problem.

The value after every assignment became the input to the next assignment. Once I stopped skipping loop bodies, the final number matched.
